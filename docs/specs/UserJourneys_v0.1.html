Concrete journeys for the persona, each anchored to a rung of the Competency Ladder — including the two users the core loop quietly assumes away: the absolute zero-verb beginner below the floor, and the user too anxious to attempt a mission at all — plus the model showing how every step compounds into real-world value.

J1

▸ First run — the panic moment

The day someone downloads the app because tomorrow terrifies them.

First runEveryday · live path

"I have to buy groceries and I freeze at the till."

Daniela · arrived 9 days ago · zero Spanish · dreads the Mercadona checkout

  1. Opens the app anxious. Instead of a placement test, she just asks for what she needs: "paying at the supermarket."
  2. It's an everyday topic, so the runtime generates the scenario live — the real checkout script, including "¿Quieres bolsa?" — no waiting, no warehouse.
  3. She drills the 3–4 gating verbs with card/tile assembly first (no keyboard wall), hears native-style audio, sees the rhythm.
  4. She gets a Real-World Mission: go buy one thing, decline the bag.
  5. At the store she gets through it clutching her phone — and self-grades Rung 1: survived with phone.
Value she gotThe terror had a target. She walked in with the exact words the cashier would use — not a textbook sentence — and came out having done it once. The Fragment Trap loosened its grip.
LADDER: 0 → 1 · froze → survived with phone
J2

▸ High-stakes — the appointment that matters

Where getting the phrasing wrong has real consequences — so the app guards it.

High-stakes · vetted path

"I have a cita at the extranjería on Tuesday."

Marcus · 6 weeks in · digital-nomad visa · TIE appointment looming

  1. He asks to rehearse his immigration appointment. The topic flags high-stakes, so it routes to the vetted path, not free generation.
  2. The dialogue uses the validated phrasing layer — correct, current bureaucratic terms (cita previa, huella, resguardo), native-reviewed. No hallucinated officialese.
  3. He drills the verbs he'll actually need (recoger, presentar, esperar) and rehearses the full exchange with the "funcionario."
  4. The runtime throws a curveball: the official asks for a document he didn't expect. He has to improvise.
  5. Real appointment: he handles the expected questions solo and copes when one goes off-script. Self-grades Rung 3: handled a curveball.
Value he gotHe walked into a consequential, intimidating setting prepared with phrasing he can trust — and didn't collapse when reality deviated from the script. That trust is exactly what the tiered model exists to protect.
LADDER: → 3 · handled a curveball · WERE YOU UNDERSTOOD? understood, repeated once
J3

▸ Daily coach — the same shop, leveling up

The scenario doesn't retire. It becomes a living surface because the interaction recurs in real life.

Daily coachLadder climb

"I go to the same panadería every morning."

Daniela · now week 5 · returning daily · verbs compounding

  1. The bakery scenario she once "survived" re-serves with less scaffolding — she's now typing the verbs freely, not tapping tiles (scaffold has faded with mastery).
  2. Verbs she first learned at the supermarket carry over from the shared mastery pool — querer, dar, pagar already feel automatic.
  3. The runtime generates a fresh small-talk variant: the baker comments on the weather, asks how her morning's going.
  4. Mission: actually make the small talk, not just transact. She does — a real, unscripted exchange.
  5. Self-grades Rung 4: made small talk. The baker now greets her by name.
Value she gotShe stopped dreading the interaction and started enjoying it. Survival became belonging — the top of the ladder, and the reason she keeps opening the app every day.
LADDER: 2 → 4 · survived solo → made small talk · the daily-coach habit forms
J5

▸ Below the floor — the absolute zero-verb beginner

Day one, no Spanish at all. The core loop assumes you can already "drill the gating verbs" — but this person has an empty pool. There's nothing to gate a scenario with yet.

Cold startNow in PRD v0.4

"I don't know a single verb. Where do I even begin?"

Tomás · landed yesterday · zero Spanish · can't yet assemble even one scenario

  1. He opens the app with nothing in the shared verb pool. A full scenario like "grocery checkout" needs 3–4 verbs he doesn't have — the loop can't start where the other journeys start.
  2. What happens (PRD v0.4 §5.0): he's routed to micro-scenarios — tiny one-verb situations, not abstract drills. querer lives inside "point at the pastry and say you want it"; tener inside "tell them you have a card." Even at the floor, a verb never appears as a naked conjugation table.
  3. At true zero, the generation-over-recognition rule relaxes: he meets each verb by recognising it first (hear it, match it, see it in its tiny context) — you can't generate what you've never encountered. Recognition is the on-ramp; it's the rung below the cards on the same scaffold-and-fade ramp.
  4. As each micro-scenario is passed, that verb enters the shared pool. The micro-scenarios are authored once (a system-generated corpus), so there's always a tiny situation for the next core verb.
  5. After ~15–20 core verbs are seeded, micro-scenarios compose into full ones — and only now does he flow into the standard loop (drill → rehearse → mission) the other journeys describe.
Value he gotHe had a first day that was actually possible. Instead of bouncing off a scenario he had no vocabulary to operate, he built the seed pool through tiny, winnable, situated moments — and arrived at his first real scenario already holding the verbs it needs.
Resolved · PRD v0.4 §5.0 + §4.3The loop's step 2 ("drill the gating verbs") presupposed a non-empty pool. PRD v0.4 now defines the cold-start: (1) a micro-scenario seeding phase for the first ~15–20 core verbs (one-verb situations, system-authored once); (2) an explicit recognition rung below the cards in the scaffold-and-fade ladder, framing generation as the destination, not the entry point; (3) a graduation rule for when micro-scenarios compose into full scenarios. Encoded in the seed_verb_pool contract.
LADDER: pre-entry · seeding the pool → first full scenario becomes operable
J4

▸ Not ready — the user who can't face the mission yet

The most fragile and most likely to churn. For them, the real-world mission is the wall — so value has to flow before they ever attempt one.

Pre-missionNow in PRD v0.4

"I know the words. I still can't make myself walk in."

Aïcha · 3 weeks in · high anxiety · has rehearsed the café scenario four times but never gone

  1. She returns to the app daily but keeps re-doing rehearsals instead of taking missions. The "Go do it for real" prompt makes her close the app.
  2. She's not failing the language — she's avoiding the exposure. A binary "survived / didn't" mission offers her no safe rung to stand on, so she stalls at the threshold.
  3. What happens (PRD v0.4 §4.5): the app meets her before the real world. She role-plays the full café exchange with the AI partner as many times as she wants — the partner can fluff its lines, slow down, let her retry with zero stakes.
  4. The ladder gains a pre-mission rung — "Rehearsed it confidently" — and the mission screen offers an explicit "Not yet — let me practice once more" instead of only survived/froze. Confidence is allowed to build in private.
  5. After several stakes-free reps she chooses, on her own terms, to try the smallest possible real version (order one coffee, nothing else). Only now does she approach Rung 1.
Value she gotShe stayed. The app gave her somewhere to be before she was brave enough for the street — and let her measure progress as growing confidence, not just real-world wins she wasn't ready to attempt. Without this, she'd have churned silently.
Resolved · PRD v0.4 §4.5 + §5.3The loop no longer jumps straight from rehearsal to a binary mission. PRD v0.4 adds (1) a "Rehearsed confidently" pre-mission rung below 0, (2) unlimited stakes-free AI role-play as a first-class surface (loop step 3), and (3) a "Not yet" mission option explicitly not scored as failure. Encoded in the rehearse_dialogue (unscored) and grade_mission (not_yet) contracts.
LADDER: pre-0 (rehearsed confidently) → eventually 1 · confidence before exposure
VF

⟳ How value flows

Each step of the loop produces a distinct gain — and the gains compound, not reset.

Seed
If the pool is empty: micro-scenarios seed the first ~15–20 core verbs (recognition-first)→ Gain: a possible first day. Even at zero, verbs arrive inside tiny real situations, never as drills (see J5).
↓
Ask
User names a real situation they're afraid of→ Gain: relevance. The app starts from their tomorrow, not a syllabus. No friction, no placement test.
↓
Surface
Runtime serves the scenario — live, or vetted if high-stakes→ Gain: the exact local script (the words the cashier/funcionario will really use), with trust calibrated to the stakes.
↓
Drill
Gating verbs, scaffold-and-fade (tiles → word-bank → typing)→ Gain: rapid retrieval being built without the keyboard wall. Effort titrated to mastery.
↓
Rehearse
Full dialogue + native/synth audio, adaptive role-play — repeatable, zero stakes→ Gain: the ear tuned to real Castellano speed, and a safe place to build confidence before exposure. For the most anxious user, this is where value flows first (see J4).
↓
Mission
Do it for real — or "not yet." Self-grade on the ladder + "Were you understood?"→ Gain: a real-world win banked, or permission to practice once more without it counting as failure. Confidence from doing, on the user's own timeline.
↓
Level up
Same scenario re-serves with curveballs & small talk→ Gain: a path beyond survival — toward handling the unexpected, and toward belonging.
⟲   Mastered verbs flow into the shared pool → the next scenario starts ahead, not from zero. Effort compounds.
THE THROUGH-LINE → value is not "lessons completed." It's a ladder of real interactions survived, then mastered, then enjoyed — each one banking confidence and verbs that make the next interaction easier. Frozen → survived → belonging.
